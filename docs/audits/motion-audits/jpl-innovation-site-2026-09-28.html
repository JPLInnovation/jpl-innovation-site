The motion is well built and safe: springs without bounce, transform and opacity only, and reduced motion fully honoured. The problem was quantity. 02

Where the timings land

Interaction feedback sits in the fast zones. The scroll reveals use a 0.9 s spring with no bounce. Most of the visible movement happens in the first ~400 ms, and each block plays it once per visit. That's acceptable for a rare entrance, and it's why the number of moments matters more than their length.

The animations

  1. 1Press feedback (scale 0.97)120ms
  2. 2Card link arrow gap150ms
  3. 3Card hover lift + shadow300ms
  4. 4Hero title + intro reveal0.9s spring
  5. 5Card row / team reveal0.9s spring
Instant / Responsive · 0–300ms — taps, presses, hovers.
Deliberate · 300–500ms — larger surfaces and lifts.
Sluggish · 500ms+ — only for rare, one-time moments.
What's off

Nothing in the fast zones. Dots 4 and 5 are long, but they play once and settle early. The issue was how many there were: eleven reveals before the fix, three after.

Every block of a reading page rose into place, including the story paragraphs

WhatAs first drafted, the page put data-reveal or data-reveal-group on eleven blocks: each section heading, the four story paragraphs (staggered), the card row, the team and the values. Four separate lists staggered in turn.

Why it mattersThis is the "stagger on every list" and "same entrance on every section" pattern. On a page meant to be read, text that slides in makes the reader wait at every scroll, and when everything moves, nothing stands out.

Recommended motionReading text and headings are simply there. Keep one rise for the project card row (the work is what should arrive), plus the hero and the team cards from the shared components. Result: three moments, not eleven.
