## Each moment: technique and cost

Measured today on Home (headless, medium tier, for the contact scene's frame): 34 draw calls, about 2.5k triangles,
**8 lights**. The drone alone measured 93 draw calls and 73k triangles in the drone chapter. I'll measure the title
scene before coding; you measure the frame rate on the Iris Xe.

| Moment | Technique | Estimated cost |
|---|---|---|
| Bench and rim light | The studio's own stage and room (no second GPU context). The desk drone is a clone of the "drones" station's drone (`stations.ts`): `Object3D.clone()` shares its geometry; its materials are cloned (cheap) so the dissolve can't touch the station's drone. Rim: the stage's own camera-carried rim light, re-levelled during the hook (no new light) | +93 draw calls and 73k triangles while the hook plays; 0 new lights |
| Dissolve | An `onBeforeCompile` patch on the cloned materials (the pattern `showcase.ts` already uses): a noise threshold that discards fragments top to bottom with a glowing edge | A few shader operations per drone pixel; no textures |
| Particles (max 4,500) | One `Points` draw call. Points sampled once from the `.bin`'s triangles, weighted by area, coloured from the material under them; every motion (dissolve release, drift, collapse, blueprint) is in the vertex shader, phase-driven by one uniform | 1 draw call, no CPU per frame; falls to about 1,500 points on the low tier |
| The glowing line | The same points squeezed onto a line, plus one soft additive sprite (a fake glow, no post-processing) | +1 draw call |
| Blueprint | The particles move to the edges of the 68 part boxes stored in the `.bin`; a matching `LineSegments` fades in | +1 draw call (about 800 segments) |
| Reassembly, part by part | The showcase's exploded-view offsets, reversed, with a delay per connected piece, so hundreds of real pieces seat in a cascade; the dissolve threshold runs backwards with it | Shader only |
| Lamp, logo, slogan | The studio's own lamp ramp, logo and slogan (`room.ts`) | None new |
| Blueprint lines on the wall | One additive plane on the wall by the desk: the F450's part boxes drawn front-on as lines (real geometry, not an invented drawing), brightness tied to the lamp | +1 draw call, one canvas texture made at load (about 1024 x 512) |
| Ho Chi Minh City night, open space | See "The backdrop" | +3-4 draw calls |

## The backdrop: an ordinary Ho Chi Minh City night, open space

- **A wide curved backdrop behind the room**: three open cylinder segments (inside-facing) at different radii, 15 to
  40 m out, so the camera's pull-back gives a slight parallax. Far: a dark sky with a faint orange haze (a gradient in
  the shader, no texture). Middle and near: building silhouettes with randomly lit windows in warm white and amber,
  drawn in code into two canvas textures at load (2048 x 512 each; no download). No neon, no cyan or magenta.
- **Traffic**: one `Points` draw call of about 150 small red and white lights, moving along a few road lines at the
  foot of the near layer in the vertex shader (no CPU per frame).
- **Your own night photo instead**, if you provide one: blurred, EXIF stripped, WebP under 60 KB, on the far layer.
- **Light theme**: the room is dark while the hook plays in both themes, so the night is the same. After the lamp, in
  the light theme, the backdrop tints to the dusk the back window already shows there (sky deep blue to violet with an
  orange glow at the horizon, fewer lit windows, slate silhouettes): one uniform, same textures.
- **Cost**: 3 draw calls for the layers + 1 for the traffic; about 8 MB of GPU memory for the two 2048 x 512 textures.

**Conflict to decide (1).** The room is a closed box: walls and a ceiling (`room.ts`, x -8.5 to 8.5, z -3.4 to 6.4,
3.4 m high), and the title shot looks at the front wall, where the logo and slogan hang. A backdrop "behind the whole
room" can't be seen through solid walls. Options:
- **(a) Recommended:** the front wall left of the desk becomes floor-to-ceiling glass, with the logo and slogan on a
  wall pier above the desk as today. That changes Home's room in every scene that faces that wall (title, sketch,
  research, the first stations), in both modes and themes.
- **(b)** The same, but the glass shows only while the hook plays (a solid wall again afterwards): Home stays as it is,
  but the room changes under the camera at the hand-off.
- **(c)** Only through the existing back-wall window: a framed window, which you said you don't want.

## Engine change, behind a flag

The hook needs the studio's engine to run its own clock while the room waits (today `html.studio-dark` holds the lamp
and the clock), and to let the hook move the camera. That is a change to `src/lib/studio/engine.ts` (the Home engine),
so it goes behind one flag (`hook3d`), off unless the hook is playing. **Regression check:** with the hook not playing
(`hook-seen` set), screenshot all 12 studio scenes through `?studio=capture` in both themes, and Classic Home in both
themes, before and after; the pixels must match apart from the room's own slow motion (a small threshold). Run on every
commit of this branch.

reassembled drone is the real CAD. The skyline is generic (drawn), not a claim about real buildings, unless your photo
