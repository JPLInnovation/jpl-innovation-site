Assemble

  1. Clear any stringing from the holes. The hinge holes are 3.3 mm (the bolt slides through); the lock holes are 2.6 mm and the board posts 1.8 mm, so the screws cut their own threads.
  2. Check the ribbon is seated in the camera: blue side facing away from the lens, latch closed. Slide the board into the case from the back, lens through the window, connector at the bottom, ribbon out through the notch.
  3. Four M2 × 6 screws through the board's corner holes into the posts. Snug only: they bite into plastic, and the board is thin.
  4. Continuity-test the bottom plate's two front slots first (guide step 2.3): the beeper must stay silent from each slot edge to every + and − pad. Then the base goes under the front tab, numbers on the arms facing outward: an M3 × 12 bolt with a washer down through each slot, through the base, nyloc nut underneath.
  5. Lift the case between the arms with its ears inside them, line up the holes and push the M3 × 45 bolt through arm, ear, ear, arm. Washer and nyloc nut on the far side. Tighten until the camera holds its angle but still turns with a firm push.
  6. An M3 × 8 screw through the arc slot into each side of the case. Turn slowly the first time while it cuts its thread.

Set the angle

  1. Loosen both lock screws half a turn (and the hinge nut a quarter turn if it's stiff).
  2. Tilt the camera. The lock screw's centre points at the angle: long ticks at 0°, 20° and 40° have numbers, short ticks are 10° and 30°, and the last short tick is 45°.
  3. Tighten both lock screws, then the hinge nut. Check the live view on the dashboard.
TiltUseHorizon, standardHorizon, wide

Horizon = how far down from the top edge of the picture the horizon sits when the quad hovers level. "Above" means the horizon is out of the picture. Your Camera Module 3 is the standard lens unless its box says Wide; rpicam-hello --list-cameras on the Pi prints imx708_wide for the wide one.

Flying forward tips the whole drone nose-down by roughly 5–15°, and the camera tips with it, so the picture looks further down than in a hover. If you mostly fly forward, use less tilt than for hovering: 0–10°.

Fit on the F450

  • The hinge sits 17 mm under the bottom plate and 94 mm ahead of its centre. The lens ends up 2 cm ahead of the plate's front edge.
  • Props stay out of the picture at every tilt: the nearest prop tip is more than 50° above the lens, and the top of the picture is at most 34° above it (wide lens at 0°).
  • The landing legs sit behind the lens, so they never appear in the picture either.
  • The Pi case goes right behind the mount, fan and camera slot facing down, HDMI and USB-C side toward the rear. At 45° the case's lower back edge still clears the Pi case by 6 mm.
  • The ribbon runs back under the Pi case and up into its camera slot, about 5 cm behind the case's front edge. That uses about 9 cm, a little less as the camera tilts down. Fold the rest of the 20 cm ribbon flat under the Pi case and tape it, or fit a 15 cm Raspberry Pi camera cable (15-pin, 1 mm pitch).
  • No slots in your plate's front tab? Two 2.5 mm cable ties through the base's two small slots and around the tab also work, with a strip of foam tape in between. Bolts are stiffer, so the picture shakes less.
Bolts through the bottom plate: the plate is a circuit board carrying battery voltage. Never drill it, and continuity-test each slot first.
