Before you start

This edition (16 October 2026) matches the quad as it is built now. Every table, drawing and command below uses these parts, and the 3D layout and the camera mount page show the same build.

What changed since 13 October
  • Camera: a Raspberry Pi Camera Module 3 on a 3D-printed tilt mount (0–45° down) replaces the Logitech C920-C. It is 142 g lighter, plugs into the Pi's CAMERA connector with a ribbon, and MediaMTX reads it directly at 1280 × 720, 60 fps.
  • Layout under the frame: camera at the very front, the Pi 4 case right behind it (lid and fan down), the battery under the rear half. The FC's USB cable now uses a black USB 2.0 port; both blue USB 3.0 ports stay empty.
  • Landing gear: white high F450 legs, 150 mm, about 90 g for the set, 8 × M2.5 screws.
  • Warnings: a 12 mm active 5 V buzzer on the FC and a 1–8S LiPo alarm on the balance lead.
  • Software: camera setup with rpicam-hello, MediaMTX's rpiCamera source, 4G on the MF833 with Viettel's APN, sharing the dashboard with a friend or publicly through Tailscale Funnel, and reader v2 straight from the repo.

Props off

Every test before step 13.8 happens with the props off: soldering, configuring, motor tests and failsafe tests. A 10-inch prop on an A2212 at full throttle cuts to the bone.

LiPo
  • Charge in balance mode at 1C (2.2 A for the 2200 mAh pack), inside the LiPo bag, on a non-flammable surface. Stay with it.
  • Store packs at 3.8 V per cell. Never discharge below 3.0 V per cell. Don't leave a pack in the sun or a parked car; Saigon heat is enough to damage it.
  • A puffed, dented or hot pack is finished. Don't charge it again.
  • The LiPo alarm draws a little current all the time: unplug it from the balance lead after every flight.
Polarity

Reversed + and − on the XT60, the switch, the IO board's battery pads, the BEC or the Pi's 5 V kills parts instantly. Check every new power joint with the multimeter before applying power, and do the first power-up through a smoke stopper (step 4.9).

Flying legally in Vietnam

At about 1.2 kg this quad sits in the 0.25–2 kg class of Decree 288/2025/NĐ-CP. The drone must be registered, the pilot must be 18 or older with a Class A certificate (required from 1 July 2026), and each flight needs a permit from the Ministry of National Defence. Check no-fly zones at cambay.mod.gov.vn. Fines reach 40 million VND plus confiscation. Everything up to step 13.6 is bench work and needs no permit; for the first hover, a certified adult can be pilot in command. Summarised from DJI Vietnam's guide to the decree; confirm with the ministry or a licensed drone school before flying.

Layout plan

The rule for this build: flight-critical and magnetically sensitive parts go on top, the payload hangs underneath, and the radios sit as far apart as the frame allows. Under the frame, the camera and the Pi 4 sit at the front and the battery at the rear. Worked out from the part weights, they balance within about 5 mm of the centre, so the battery hardly needs sliding.

Radio and compass rules

  • Receiver antennas at 90°. Antenna A lies flat along the top of the front-right arm in a tube, its tip at least 2 cm from the motor wires and never pointing up into the prop disc. Antenna B stands vertical in a tube on the top plate. Keep both active tips (the last 3 cm) clear of the FC, power wires and anything metal or carbon.
  • 4G modem away from the receiver. The MF833 hangs on the rear-left leg, diagonally opposite and below the receiver, with the PDB's copper between them.
  • Compass away from current and magnets. The onboard QMC5883 sits about 2 cm above the PDB, so this guide switches it off in INAV. The external compass (upgrade) goes on a 10–15 cm mast at the front left, at least 15 cm from the Pi case, the modem and the battery leads. The Pi's fan motor magnet, steel screws and current loops are what disturb a compass.
  • Quiet the other 2.4 GHz transmitters. The FC's Bluetooth module sits right next to the receiver: use it on the bench if you like, then switch it off for flying (hold BOOT for about 2 s to toggle it; the Bluetooth status LED shows the state). Check whether it stays off after a power cycle. On the Pi, Bluetooth is disabled in step 9.4 and Wi-Fi goes off for flights. Only the two black USB 2.0 ports are used (FC cable and modem): USB 3.0 radiates noise across the 2.4 GHz band, so both blue ports stay empty.

Cable-tie points

  • ESC: two ties per arm, one at the ESC and one at the arm root, catching both the power and signal leads.
  • Motor wires: one tie under the motor so the bullets can't flap.
  • Antenna A: two ties holding its tube flat on the arm. Antenna B: tube tied to a nylon standoff.
  • Camera ribbon: taped flat to the underside of the Pi case, the spare length folded in round folds. Leave 1–2 cm of slack at the camera so it can tilt the full 0–45°. Never tie it tight or crease it.
  • MF833 and its extension: two ties along the outside of the rear-left leg, USB end up; one more tie where the extension leaves the Pi case.
  • FC-to-Pi USB cable: through the rear slot of the top plate, tied to a standoff, with a loose loop so a tug can't reach the USB-C plug, then along the left edge to the Pi.
  • Battery lead and XT60: tied down beside the battery so the plug can't swing.
  • LiPo alarm: hook-and-loop on the battery's right side; its balance lead tucked under the battery strap.

  1. Lay the parts in their zones with masking tape. Check every cable reaches: the 25 cm USB cable from the FC's USB-C port to a black USB port on the Pi's left end, the BEC-to-Pi USB-C cable (10–15 cm), the 12 AWG IO feed from plate pair IO to the IO board (10–12 cm), the ESC signal leads to the IO board, the CR4321VG cable from the IO board to the FC (it must reach the FC's spot behind the IO board with a little slack, no tension), the 20 cm modem extension to the rear-left leg, and the camera ribbon from the front tab to the Pi case's camera slot (about 9 cm).

  2. Write FRONT on tape on the top plate. If your frame has coloured arms, they're the front pair.

  3. The bottom plate is a circuit board. Before you put a screw, bolt or strap through a hole or slot, test with the multimeter's continuity beep from its edge to the + pad and to the − pad: both must stay silent. Do this now for the battery-strap slots and the two front-tab slots the camera mount will use. Never drill the bottom plate.

Verify
  • With everything taped in place and the battery strapped, lift the quad by two fingertips under the arm roots: it hangs nearly level.
  • Seen from above, nothing sits inside a prop disc except the arms, motors and antenna A lying on its arm.
  • Tape-measure from the receiver to the MF833: at least 15 cm.
  • The strap slots and both front-tab slots stayed silent on the continuity test.

Frame & motors

Mechanics first, while the bottom plate is still open. INAV numbers the motors 1 rear-right, 2 front-right, 3 rear-left, 4 front-left (Figure 1A), and with the default props-in setup the front-left and rear-right motors spin clockwise.

  1. Front arms forward, arm roots flush with the plate. The M2.5 screws go into plastic: snug them, don't crank them, and no threadlocker on plastic. Leave the top plate off for now.

  2. "2 CW, 2 CCW" refers to the prop-adapter thread, and sellers label it inconsistently, so test instead of trusting the label. Thread a prop nut onto each adapter by hand. The rule: the nut must tighten when you turn it opposite to the motor's spin, so air drag keeps it tight.

    • Front-left and rear-right spin clockwise: they get the two motors whose nut tightens anticlockwise (reverse thread).
    • Front-right and rear-left spin anticlockwise: they get the two whose nut tightens clockwise (normal thread).

    Label each motor with its corner.

  3. Four M3 screws per motor, wires pointing along the arm. After each screw, spin the bell by hand: rubbing or a gritty feel means the screw touches the windings, so swap in a shorter one. A drop of medium threadlocker is right here (metal into metal).

  4. Two cable ties per ESC, power leads toward the centre, motor leads toward the motor. Label them ESC 1 rear-right, ESC 2 front-right, ESC 3 rear-left, ESC 4 front-left. These numbers match INAV's motor numbers and the IO board headers.

  5. Plug the three 3.5 mm bullets in any order and push them fully home; a half-seated bullet causes desyncs in flight. Spin direction gets fixed in step 8.9.

  6. One white arched leg under each arm, right next to the bottom plate, its foot pointing outward along the arm (Figure 1C). Each leg takes two of the kit's M2.5 screws up through its top bracket into the arm's two holes nearest the plate. Check which holes line up on your arms before you screw anything: on most F450 copies they are the arm's own lower holes, and the leg kit's longer screws replace the short ones there. Snug them into the plastic, no threadlocker.

    • Stand the frame on its legs on a flat table: all four feet touch and the plate sits level. If one rocks, loosen its two screws, press the frame down and tighten again.
    • Legs go on before the payload so you can set the frame down while you work underneath it.
Verify
  • Every bell spins freely by hand with no rubbing.
  • Arms don't wobble when you twist them.
  • Each motor's nut tightens opposite to that corner's spin (Figure 1A).
  • ESC labels match: 1 rear-right, 2 front-right, 3 rear-left, 4 front-left.
  • The frame stands level on all four legs without rocking.

Do all the power soldering now, with the top plate still off. The battery current splits once, on the bottom plate: pair IO sends it to the IO board, which feeds the four ESCs and the FC, and pair U sends a small share to the Matek BEC for the Pi. 05

Pi power

The Raspberry Pi 4, its Camera Module 3 and the MF833 run from their own Matek BEC12S-PRO, fed from bottom-plate pair U. The flight controller and the Pi are linked by one short USB cable (data, ground and USB's 5 V pin) and nothing else. Neither supplies the other's loads.

Why not the flight controller's 5 V BEC

  1. Not enough current. The Pi stack needs about 1.7 A at 5 V when streaming and up to about 2.6 A at peaks (table below). The FC's 5 V BEC is rated 3 A in total and already feeds the FC itself and the receiver, plus a GPS later. A modem transmit burst would drag that rail down and the flight controller could brown out in the air.
  2. The plug can't carry it. The FC's 5V pins are 1.0 mm JST-SH contacts rated about 1 A, and its USB-C port isn't built for a load either. Two to three amps through either heats the crimp and drops the voltage, so the Pi would undervolt anyway.
  3. Noise and isolation. Modem bursts and the Pi's load steps would ride on the same 5 V that feeds the receiver and the FC's sensors. With its own BEC, a short or crash on the Pi side can't take the flight controller with it.

The BEC: Matek BEC12S-PRO

  • Recommended by a professional drone builder in Vietnam. 9–55 V input (3–12S), so it keeps working if you move to a bigger pack later.
  • 5 A continuous and 9 A peak, against the Pi stack's 2.6 A peak.
  • The output voltage is not chosen automatically: two solder pads set it (Figure 2C). Both open = 5 V, which is what the Pi needs. A solder blob across the 8V pad gives 8 V and across the 12V pad gives 12 V, for FPV gear. Either one on the Pi's USB-C input destroys it. Measure before connecting (step 5.1).
  • Measure 5.10–5.30 V before the Pi is connected (step 5.1). The Pi's undervoltage alarm trips below about 4.63 V, so the extra tenths cover the cable.
  • Nothing goes on the EN pad. If the output reads 0 V in step 5.1, read Matek's leaflet about the EN pad before anything else.
  • A generic 5 A UBEC also works if it measures 5.10–5.30 V. Avoid 3 A ones: too close to the Pi's peak.

    EZ Tune at Filter 75 Hz is a sensible start for 10-inch props on this frame. Leave the other EZ Tune sliders at their defaults until after the first hover.

    INAV keeps three control profiles, and the baseline and your tuning live in profile 1. Configurator can leave you on another one (it did on this build: the saved diff ended with control_profile 3). The profile dropdown in the top bar must be on 1; set it there if not.

    • UART1: leave it as the target set it. Nothing is plugged in; the Pi talks MSP through the USB port, which has no switch here and always works.
    • UART6: Serial RX on and MSP off. The receiver's SBUS lives here.
    • Serial RX off on every other UART. INAV takes a single serial receiver, so a stray Serial RX switch on UART5 or UART7 can starve UART6. Also leave MSP off on UART7: it is the ESC-telemetry port, which this build doesn't use.
    • Everything else stays off for now. Configurator reaches the FC over the air through the Pi (step 10.5), so no extra UART is needed for that. Later, UART3 becomes the GPS port when the GPS arrives.

    Click Save and Reboot. Check that the switches are still as you set them after the reboot: if INAV rejects a combination it resets the ports, and then you redo UART6 and save again.

    • Sensors: magnetometer None (from the baseline), barometer Auto (the onboard SPL06).
    • Board orientation: all zeros when the FC arrow points forward. If you had to turn the board, enter the yaw angle you turned it clockwise.
    • Battery: 3 cells, voltage scale 2112. Battery in (props off), measure the pack at the XT60 with the multimeter and compare with INAV's reading (Setup tab, or status in the CLI). New scale = 2112 × meter ÷ INAV. Save and repeat until they agree within 0.05 V.
    • Current meter: off. The F450 PDB has no current sensor.
    • Receiver type Serial, provider SBUS, channel map AETR.
    • Transmitter on. Roll right, pitch forward (right stick up), throttle up and yaw right must each push their bar toward 2000. A backwards channel gets reversed on the MC6C, not in INAV.
    • Ends near 1000 and 2000 (this receiver's SBUS range 200–1800 lands at about 1005–2005 in INAV), centres near 1500; use the MC6C trims.
    • CH5 is the 3-position switch: three steps near 1005, 1505 and 2005. CH6 is a 2-position switch on the standard MC6C and a knob on the MC6C V2.

    No bars move at all? Work down this list: receiver bound (6.7)? Signal wire on RX6, not TX6? 5 V reaching the receiver? Only then try the other polarity: set serialrx_inverted = ON, save, check again. If that changes nothing, set it back to OFF. Standard SBUS is inverted and the H743's UART inverts it internally, so OFF is the normal setting.

    • ARM on AUX1 (CH5), range 1300–2100. Only the 1005 end disarms; the middle (1505) and the 2005 end arm. The range must include 1505, because that is what the receiver sends on every channel when the signal is lost (8.8): with a range that starts at 1700, the quad would disarm in the air and the motors would stop at once instead of landing. On this MC6C the 2005 end was the lever's down position. Read yours on the Receiver tab and set the range by value, not by the words up and down.
    • INAV refuses to arm if CH5 is already in an arming position when the quad powers up or the radio comes back. Put CH5 at 1005, wait a few seconds, then arm. The Arming Flags in the status bar at the very bottom of Configurator (not a tab) list what blocks arming.
    • ANGLE on AUX1 across the whole range 900–2100: self-levelling is always on for the first flights. Check that the row exists: on this build only ARM had been assigned, which means acro mode and a flip on the first hover. Add Range → ANGLE if it is missing, then Save.
    • Later, once nav_mc_hover_thr is set, you can add NAV ALTHOLD on CH6 if yours is a 2-position switch.

  8. Procedure LAND: INAV levels the quad and descends at 2 m/s using the barometer, then turns the motors off when it detects landing or after 20 s. The catch: this receiver doesn't stop transmitting when the signal goes. Measured on this build: with the transmitter off, roll, pitch, yaw and every switch channel jump to 1505 and throttle drops to 905. That looks like a valid signal, because INAV only calls a channel invalid below rx_min_usec (default 885), so INAV never noticed the loss and the dashboard (10.6) kept saying 100 %.

    1. Receiver tab, transmitter on: note the throttle reading with the stick fully down (about 1005).
    2. Switch the transmitter off: all bars go to 1505 and throttle to 905, and the Arming Flags in the bottom bar show no radio problem. That is the failure.
    3. CLI: set rx_min_usec = 950, save (the baseline already has it). Check that no channel's normal low end drops below 950 (the lowest readings on this build were about 1005).
    4. Repeat with the transmitter off, disarmed: throttle now holds its last good value and the Arming Flags show ARMING_DISABLED_RC_LINK within about a second. With the transmitter back on, the flags clear after a few seconds; if ARMING_DISABLED_ARM_SWITCH stays, put CH5 at 1005 first.
    5. Armed test, props off, ARM range 1300–2100 (8.7): arm, raise throttle to about 20–30 %, switch the transmitter off. The motors must keep spinning and not cut out, because INAV has taken over with the LAND failsafe. Props off, the quad can't level itself, so one motor climbs (we saw 76 %) while another falls to near 0 %: that is normal without props. After about 20 s INAV disarms by itself (we saw 21 s).

    Never set a receiver failsafe that holds mid throttle: a held 1500 µs makes the quad climb away.

    Lesson from the first run: before this fix the transmitter-off test seemed to pass because the motors stopped. That was the arm switch falling to 1505 and disarming under the old 1700 range. A real failsafe would have dropped the quad. After any change to ARM or rx_min_usec, repeat the armed test. Also run get nav_emerg_landing_speed: this FC still had the default 500 (5 m/s) instead of the baseline's 200.

    • Mixer: Multirotor, Quad X, motor direction normal (props-in).

    • Wrong motor turns: that ESC's lead is on the wrong header. Wrong direction: swap any two of that motor's three bullets (BLHeliSuite or ESC-Configurator through INAV's passthrough also works).
    • Nothing spins on DShot300? Older BLHeli_S firmware may not speak DShot: set motor_pwm_protocol = ONESHOT125, save, and calibrate the ESCs props off: battery unplugged, master slider to maximum, plug the battery in, wait for the beeps, master slider to minimum.

Telemetry test

drone-telemetry-cpp talks to INAV over USB, on the FC's /dev/serial/by-id/... port from step 9.4. The USB port always speaks MSP and ignores the baud rate (115200 is harmless). MSP is request and response, so INAV only answers what you ask. The FC's USB-C serves one computer at a time: with the Pi's cable in, your laptop can't use it directly, so either unplug the Pi's cable or configure through the Pi without unplugging anything (step 10.5).

  1. A 40-line probe that asks INAV for its name, version, battery and live attitude. If this works, any problem left is in the reader, not the wiring.

    Pi · ~/msp_probe.py
    #!/usr/bin/env python3
"""Minimal MSP v1 probe for INAV over USB (sudo apt install python3-serial)."""
import glob, struct, sys, time
import serial

ports = glob.glob("/dev/serial/by-id/*")
PORT = sys.argv[1] if len(sys.argv) > 1 else (ports[0] if len(ports) == 1 else "")
if not PORT:
    raise SystemExit("pass the FC's port; found: " + (", ".join(ports) or "nothing in /dev/serial/by-id/"))
s = serial.Serial(PORT, 115200, timeout=0.3)   # baud is ignored on USB

def msp(cmd, payload=b""):
    size = len(payload)
    crc = size ^ cmd
    for b in payload:
        crc ^= b
    s.write(b"$M<" + bytes([size, cmd]) + payload + bytes([crc]))
    hdr = s.read(5)
    if len(hdr) < 5 or hdr[:3] != b"$M>":
        raise SystemExit(f"no reply to command {cmd}: got {hdr.hex(' ') or 'nothing'}")
    n, c = hdr[3], hdr[4]
    body = s.read(n + 1)
    if len(body) != n + 1:
        raise SystemExit("reply cut short")
    data, rx_crc = body[:-1], body[-1]
    chk = n ^ c
    for b in data:
        chk ^= b
    if c != cmd or chk != rx_crc:
        raise SystemExit("bad checksum or wrong command in reply")
    return data

s.reset_input_buffer()
print("variant :", msp(2).decode(errors="replace"))            # MSP_FC_VARIANT
print("version :", ".".join(str(x) for x in msp(3)[:3]))       # MSP_FC_VERSION
api = msp(1)                                                    # MSP_API_VERSION
print("MSP API :", f"{api[1]}.{api[2]}")
print("battery :", msp(110)[0] / 10, "V")                       # MSP_ANALOG
print("tilt the quad; Ctrl+C to stop")
try:
    while True:
        r, p, y = struct.unpack("<hhh", msp(108)[:6])          # MSP_ATTITUDE
        print(f"roll {r / 10:6.1f}  pitch {p / 10:6.1f}  heading {y:4d}", end="\r", flush=True)
        time.sleep(0.1)
except KeyboardInterrupt:
    print()
    Pi · bash
    sudo apt install -y python3-serial

Security, for the repo's README
  • MSP has no authentication: whatever holds the FC's serial port can also change settings or reboot the FC. Keep the reader read-only with an allow-list of command IDs.
  • Don't leave the raw serial port bridged to the network. The bridge in 10.5 is a deliberate bench-only exception: Tailscale address only, started by hand, stopped before flight. Everything the reader and dashboard do is read-only: parse on the Pi and send your own format over loopback or Tailscale only.
  • The dashboard has no login. The firewall (9.10) is what keeps it to your tailnet, until you switch Funnel on (10.7); switch it off again after the flight.
  • Run the reader as your normal user (already in dialout), not as root.

The honest verdict for the build as it is now

It flies with a decent margin now. Swapping the C920-C for the Camera Module 3 on its printed mount took about 140 g off. On a fresh pack in 33 °C air the thrust-to-weight ratio is about 2.6 : 1; it stays above 2 : 1 until shortly before the warning and reaches about 1.9 : 1 at 3.5 V/cell, so the quad only feels a little soft at the very end. Hover takes roughly 54–62 % throttle and 13 A, which gives about 6–6.5 minutes of real flying from the 2200 mAh pack (8 minutes of pure hover).

Weight is still what limits it, but there's little left to cut: the bare-board Pi saves 30 g. The ESCs are fine: 30 A each against about 13 A per motor at full throttle. The pack's C rating is fine: full throttle pulls about 52 A, which is 24C on 2200 mAh, and a 40C-class pack handles that without much sag (a 20–25C pack would sag hard). For more time in the air, a second 2200 mAh pack to swap in beats a bigger one (stage 12).

How these numbers are worked out
  • Thrust: about 800 g per motor on a fresh 3S pack for A2212/13T 1000KV with 1045 props (seller specification; the Suppo A2212/13 test shows 802 g at 13 A on a 10×5 prop). Hot air is thinner: thrust × (air density ratio)0.6, about −4 % at 33 °C.
  • Late in the pack: at the 3.5 V/cell warning the voltage at full throttle sags from about 11.2 V to 9.5 V; thrust follows voltage squared, so about −28 %.
  • Hover power: from the same A2212/13 test (48 W per motor at 431 g), scaled with thrust1.5, plus 22 % for budget props, ESC switching and arm blockage, plus 12 W for the Pi stack, FC and receiver; the pack averages about 11.3 V in a hover.
  • Time: 80 % of the label capacity (you land at the warning), and real flying draws about 25 % more than a still hover.
  • Hover throttle range: the square root of the hover-to-full thrust ratio, minus up to 12 % because thrust doesn't scale perfectly with throttle.

Unsafe or a real bottleneck in the current build

  • Receiver failsafe: the MC7RE keeps sending a low throttle (905 µs) after signal loss, which INAV accepts as valid until rx_min_usec is raised (8.8). Fix before any flight.
  • No warning path: without the buzzer and the LiPo alarm, INAV's battery warning reaches nobody.
  • Pi 4 on the FC's 5 V: never (stage 05).
  • Camera mount in PLA: it softens in the sun and the camera droops. Print it in PETG.
  • No GPS: LAND descends where it is and drifts with the wind. With live 4G video it's tempting to fly far; don't, until upgrade 4.
  • Crowded 2.4 GHz: the RC link shares the band with the FC's Bluetooth, the Pi's Wi-Fi and USB 3.0 noise. Bluetooth off on both, Wi-Fi off for flights; the FC cable and the modem on the black USB 2.0 ports, the blue USB 3.0 ports empty (step 13.6 tests it).
  • Funnel left on: with --bg it stays public across reboots until sudo tailscale funnel reset (10.7).
  • FC HD VTX connector: pin 1 carries the second BEC (12 V per MicoAir's documentation; ArduPilot's page lists 9 V). Never plug a 5 V device into it.
  • IO board + rail and ESC BECs: measured in 6.1; ESC red wires stay out.
  • Heat: keep packs and the Pi out of direct sun on the bench, never charge a warm pack, and store packs at 3.8 V per cell.

Pre-flight & first hover

Steps 13.1–13.6 happen on the bench with the props off. The props go on at the field, in step 13.8, and nowhere earlier.

On the bench, props off

  1. Every screw tight, every bell spins freely, nothing inside a prop disc, antenna A tied flat, shake test passes.

  2. All four sticks move the right bars the right way (8.6). It arms only with the throttle down, CH5 at 1005 disarms instantly and 1505 or 2005 arm (8.7, 8.13), it stays armed at mid throttle for over a minute (retest after nav_disarm_on_landing = OFF), and all of it is the same with the Pi's USB cable plugged in (10.4). ANGLE is assigned and the top-bar profile dropdown is on 1.

  3. Transmitter off, disarmed → ARMING_DISABLED_RC_LINK within about a second. Armed, props off → the motors keep spinning and INAV disarms by itself after about 20 s (8.8). get nav_emerg_landing_speed prints 200: this FC still had the 500 default, which is a 5 m/s landing.

  4. Right corner, right direction for all four (8.9).

  5. INAV's voltage within 0.05 V of the multimeter; the buzzer beeps on the forced warning (8.10); the LiPo alarm shows three matching cells and is set to 3.5 V (6.10). Still to do on this build: the multimeter comparison was skipped.

  6. Pi on the BEC, streaming over 4G with Wi-Fi off, FC Bluetooth off, the FC's USB cable in its black USB 2.0 port, both blue ports empty. Walk 30–50 m away with the transmitter while a friend watches the Receiver tab: the bars keep following your sticks and no failsafe appears. Then sudo poweroff the Pi: INAV keeps working normally. The dashboard's Radio link card only says ON or LOST (10.6), so the Receiver tab is your range check.

  7. Quad on the ground outdoors, dashboard open on your phone. Hold the quad level at about 1 m and check the picture: the right way up, sharp, no prop or leg in view, and the horizon where you want it (start at 15°, table in 7.8). Change the tilt with the two lock screws, battery unplugged. Tilt it through 0–45° once more: the ribbon must not pull.

At the field

  1. Permit and certified pilot sorted (stage 00), wind under about 15 km/h, no rain, open ground at least 30 m from people.

  2. 1045R on front-left and rear-right (the clockwise motors), plain 1045 on front-right and rear-left. Printed side up; the thick, rounded leading edge leads the spin. Tighten each nut by turning it opposite to its motor's spin. Turn every prop by hand: nothing touches.

  3. Full (12.6 V), strapped on its mark, balance check with two fingertips. Plug the LiPo alarm onto the balance lead: every cell 4.15 V or more, and it is set to 3.5.

  4. Transmitter on, CH5 at 1005 (the disarmed end), throttle down. Then, with the switch OFF, plug in the battery, switch ON and keep the quad still and level while the FC boots and calibrates its gyro. Give the Pi about a minute if you want the stream.

  5. Stand at least 5 m behind the quad, nose pointing away from you. Without ANGLE assigned (8.7) this hover would be in acro mode, so check that first. Arm. Raise the throttle smoothly until it gets light on its legs, then lift straight to about 1 m; hopping just above the ground is where quads flip. Hold it there for 20–30 seconds with small corrections. Land by lowering smoothly; at touchdown pull the throttle to the bottom and disarm.

    Note the throttle position in the hover (or read rcCommand[3] from the blackbox log), then in the CLI: battery_profile 1, set nav_mc_hover_thr = YOUR_VALUE, save. Altitude hold needs it later. Save a fresh diff all.

  6. Motors warm is fine; too hot to hold a finger on means oscillation, so lower EZ Tune Response by 10–20 before the next flight. Battery only warm. Check for anything that loosened. To power down: shut the Pi down first unless its card is locked (9.13), then switch OFF and unplug. Unplug the LiPo alarm from the balance lead.

  7. Next flights: a 4-minute phone timer, and land at the first warning beep. After each flight check the resting voltage: 3.7 V per cell or more means you landed with a healthy reserve.
